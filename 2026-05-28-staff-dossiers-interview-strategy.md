## Interview Strategy

### Understanding the Dynamic

This is a **panel interview with two very different stakeholders:**

- **Ryan Lee** (Head of DevRel) → Technical interviewer, will probe DevRel experience, community-building track record, and hands-on AI knowledge. He's the person you'd work with or report to.
- **Siyuan Ren** (VP, GTM & Marketing) → Executive/business interviewer, will probe strategic thinking, brand representation, communication skills, and cultural fit. She controls the marketing narrative of the company.

The JD reveals what they're really looking for: someone who can **bridge North American developer communities with a Chinese AI company**. This is a cross-cultural DevRel role — not just community management, but translation (literal and cultural), product advocacy, and market intelligence flowing both ways.

### Strategy: Interviewing with Ryan Lee (Head of DevRel)

**What he's evaluating:** Can this person actually do the job? Do they understand the developer ecosystem? Can they run events, manage communities, and translate developer feedback into product improvements?

**Key themes to hit:**

1. **North American AI community experience** — Name-drop communities you've been part of (Hugging Face, GitHub open-source projects, Discord servers, local meetups, hackathons). Be specific. "I organized X event with Y people" beats "I'm passionate about AI."

2. **DevRel philosophy** — Have a clear POV on what developer relations means: it's not just marketing to developers, it's being their advocate inside the company. Mention the feedback loop: community → product → community.

3. **Technical credibility** — He's an ML researcher. Be ready to discuss:
   - MiniMax's model architecture (M2, interleaved thinking, 230B parameters, MoE)
   - How MiniMax compares to competitors (DeepSeek, Qwen, Claude, GPT)
   - The MiniMax API and agent platform (agent.minimax.io)
   - Open-source strategy (M2 on HuggingFace/GitHub)

4. **Cross-cultural competency** — This is a Chinese company going global. Talk about bridging cultures: understanding Chinese engineering culture, translating Western developer expectations, managing time zones, etc.

5. **Ask him smart questions:**
   - "What's the biggest challenge MiniMax faces in North American developer adoption right now?"
   - "How do you currently collect and route developer feedback to the product teams?"
   - "What does success look like for this role in the first 6 months?"
   - "How does Developer Relations coordinate with the marketing team and the engineering team?"

**Watch out for:** His limited English proficiency. Speak clearly, avoid idioms, check for understanding. Don't talk over him. He may prefer technical depth in Chinese if you can switch — pay attention to his comfort level.

### Strategy: Interviewing with Siyuan Ren (VP, GTM & Marketing)

**What she's evaluating:** Does this person represent the brand well? Can they tell MiniMax's story compellingly? Do they have strategic vision for developer community growth? Are they a cultural fit for a fast-moving Chinese startup?

**Key themes to hit:**

1. **MiniMax's narrative** — Show you've done your homework. Reference:
   - The IPO (HKD 100B, 100%+ day one)
   - The M2 open-source launch
   - The "Intelligence with Everyone" mission
   - Three-pillar strategy: Coding, Workplace Productivity, Multimodal Creation
   - The Cannes film festival partnership with Gong Li
   - The earnings numbers ($79M revenue, 159% YoY, 70% international)

2. **Strategic vision for DevRel** — Don't just talk tactics. Talk about:
   - How developer relations fits into MiniMax's global GTM strategy
   - The developer → enterprise pipeline (free API → paid plans → enterprise contracts)
   - Community as moat: why developers who build on MiniMax stay on MiniMax
   - Content strategy: what kind of technical content moves the needle (benchmarks, tutorials, case studies)

3. **Communication & brand** — She's a marketing executive. Demonstrate:
   - Clear, structured thinking
   - Storytelling ability
   - Understanding of different channels (GitHub, Discord, X/Twitter, technical blogs, conferences)
   - Comfort with public speaking and representing a brand

4. **North American go-to-market** — Be specific about:
   - Which AI communities matter (Hugging Face, /r/LocalLLaMA, AI Engineer Summit, Latent Space, etc.)
   - How to enter them authentically (not just showing up with swag)
   - The competitive landscape: MiniMax vs Anthropic, OpenAI, DeepSeek, Qwen in the eyes of North American developers
   - Pricing as a wedge: M2 at 8% of Claude Sonnet price is a massive advantage

5. **Ask her smart questions:**
   - "How do you see Developer Relations contributing to MiniMax's revenue growth targets?"
   - "What's been the most effective marketing channel for MiniMax in North America so far?"
   - "How does MiniMax balance its Chinese engineering culture with global developer expectations?"
   - "What's the brand story you want developers to associate with MiniMax?"
   - "How do you measure ROI on community and developer relations investments?"

**Watch out for:** She's a high-context communicator with a Columbia/USC background. She'll evaluate presentation, confidence, and cultural fit as much as content. Don't be too casual, but don't be stiff either. She's been at MiniMax for 5 years — she bleeds the brand. Show enthusiasm for the mission.

### General Tips for Both

1. **Demo MiniMax products beforehand** — Use the MiniMax Agent (agent.minimax.io), the API, Hailuo AI video. Be able to talk about the UX, the API design, what you liked, what could be better.

2. **Know the competitive landscape cold:**
   - **Anthropic (Claude):** Best coding model, strong brand, expensive API
   - **OpenAI (GPT):** Market leader, ecosystem lock-in, most developer mindshare
   - **DeepSeek:** Strong open-source, Chinese competitor, aggressive pricing
   - **Qwen (Alibaba):** Full-stack cloud + model, agent platform (QwenCloud)
   - **MiniMax's positioning:** Cheaper than Claude, more productized than DeepSeek, agent-native from the ground up

3. **Prepare a "what would you do in 90 days" plan** — Both interviewers will appreciate a concrete, actionable vision:
   - Month 1: Audit existing channels, meet engineering teams, attend community events
   - Month 2: Launch first content series, run a mini-hackathon, establish feedback pipeline
   - Month 3: Present community insights to product, plan Q3 event calendar, grow Discord/GitHub engagement

4. **The translation requirement is real** — The JD explicitly asks for English↔Chinese technical document translation. If you can demonstrate this capability (even by referencing past work translating AI content), it's a strong signal.

5. **Be ready for the startup energy question** — MiniMax just IPO'd but still operates like a startup: fast-paced, ambiguous, direct. Have a story about thriving in chaos.

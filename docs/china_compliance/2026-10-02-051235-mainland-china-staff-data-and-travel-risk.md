## Official findings and their implications

### The governing framework includes rights, development and national security

There is no single test under which a product becomes permissible because its
creator intends to benefit Chinese people. National Security Law Article 3
sets out the overall national-security concept (总体国家安全观), including
people's security, political security and economic security. Articles 7–8
also require legality, protection of rights and coordination with development.
[National Security Law](https://www.stats.gov.cn/gk/tjfg/xgfxfg/202503/t20250310_1958929.html).
Data Security Law Articles 1, 4, 7, 13 and 28 combine lawful, beneficial data use
with protection of individual and organizational rights and national interests.
These principles guide the framework; they are not a case-specific ruling about
this product.
[Data Security Law](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2021-06/11/c_1624994566919140.htm).

The practical analysis has three overlapping parts:

| Part | Question for this product |
| --- | --- |
| Individual rights | What permits collecting and using each person's information, for which purpose, with what notice, controls and rights handling? These questions exist even if data stays in China. |
| Overseas data flows | Who collects, stores or accesses the information, in which countries, and what transfer conditions apply? Hosting abroad does not settle the underlying processing question. |
| National security and public interests | Do the actual information, collection methods or uses implicate protected interests? Individual consent does not resolve every question in this category. |

**Project assessment:** ordinary professional profiles raise a direct
personal-information question. The present evidence does not establish that our
roster constitutes state secrets or legally designated important data. A table
of people, foreign ownership or Chinese AI affiliations alone does not establish
that classification. Nor can this research rank the owner's personal enforcement
risks with confidence.

### Sending information abroad is regulated, not categorically prohibited

The March 2024 cross-border provisions expressly exempt qualifying activities
from certain assessment, standard-contract and certification requirements.
Examples include specified necessary individual-contract or employment
administration uses and certain smaller transfers of nonsensitive information.
Article 10 preserves applicable notice, consent and impact-assessment duties;
an exemption from a transfer mechanism is not an exemption from all privacy
law. Article 2 also addresses notification or publication of important-data
classifications.
[Cross-border provisions, Articles 2, 5 and 10](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2024-03/22/c_1712776611775634.htm).

**Project assessment:** helping researchers find jobs does not automatically
make a third-party research database the employer's internal human-resources
system. Do not assume that employment-related exemptions apply to it. The actual
data path matters; a foreign person reading a public page, offshore collection
and a Chinese company supplying employee records need separate factual analysis.
This report does not determine which export mechanism applies to G1.

### Helping researchers is relevant, but the product must deliver that purpose

An optional service in which researchers provide or approve professional
profiles, choose audiences and request introductions presents clearer evidence
of their participation and intended benefit than unrequested dossiers marketed
as helping them. This is a design assessment, not a statutory safe harbor.
Consent would still need to match the actual processing and could not authorize
unlawful handling of company secrets or other protected information.

For a recruiting proposal, counsel should review the actual workflow, including
candidate participation, profile visibility, recipients, correction/removal and
whether employment-service licensing rules apply. This session did not research
or resolve those additional licensing rules. Moving from editorial coverage to
recruiting would be a product-scope change, not merely a different explanation
for the same collection.

### Public availability does not settle national-security or travel risk

The U.S. State Department advisory displayed an issue date of September 4,
2026 when checked. Mainland China was Level 2, meaning increased caution. It
warns about arbitrary enforcement and exit bans, and reports that Americans
have faced detention and prosecution in connection with research or accessing
publicly available material in China.
[U.S. China Travel Advisory](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/china.html).

Article 4 of China's Counterespionage Law covers specified activities involving
state secrets, intelligence, and other documents or data concerning national
security and interests. Its wording extends beyond formally classified secrets;
it does not by itself establish that this particular collection is prohibited.
[Counterespionage Law, Article 4](https://www.gc.gov.cn/columns/8e15378c-0a26-4abf-b213-3a302d59a699/202311/10/d2b1c908-8e70-47d4-a5aa-d4ee83fd4806.html).

**Project assessment:** connecting entire technical teams, aliases, employment
changes, locations and images may attract different scrutiny from a few
published founder biographies. It could be interpreted as mapping technical
personnel depending on purpose, customers and use. This is an inference, not
evidence that authorities have assessed PushinWeight or its DeepSeek roster.
U.S. citizenship and an existing visa are not grounds for assuring safe travel.

### Personal-information obligations can apply to public and offshore data

The Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL) includes collection and storage
within processing. Article 27 permits reasonable use of lawfully public
information, subject to explicit objections and consent requirements for
significant effects on individuals. Article 6 limits collection to what the
purpose requires. Article 3 also covers specified overseas processing of
mainland individuals' information, including analysis of their behavior.
[PIPL, Articles 3–6 and 27](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2021-08/20/c_1631050028355286.htm).

**Project assessment:** public profiles and search results are not unrestricted
permission for every later use. The application to this product depends on the
actual collection, analysis, audience and operating locations. Neither an
overseas server nor purchase through a search provider establishes compliance.
Cross-border obligations require a separate assessment. The general exemptions
noted above do not determine this product's mechanism or filing requirements.

### A ten-year tourism visa has a limited permitted purpose

The Chinese Embassy in the United States identifies L visas with tourism,
M visas with business/trade, F visas with visits/exchanges and Z visas with
employment. Ten-year validity does not expand an L visa's permitted purpose.
[Embassy visa guidance, updated September 2025](https://us.china-embassy.gov.cn/chn/lsfw/zj/qz/202509/t20250920_11712381.htm).
Article 37 of the Entry and Exit Administration Law prohibits activities
inconsistent with the purpose of stay; Article 81 provides for ordered departure
in relevant circumstances.
[Entry and Exit Administration Law](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013311/content.html).

**Project assessment:** operating commercial collection, conducting research
interviews or undertaking business activity while physically in China needs an
immigration assessment. This report does not determine whether a particular
instance of remote work breaches visa conditions or prescribe a replacement
visa. Operating overseas and visiting China as a tourist present different
facts for counsel to evaluate.

### A Chinese customer or employer helps with different, limited questions

| Relationship | What it could support | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A lab buys a genuine subscription | A contract, invoices and actual deliverables can document an ordinary commercial purpose. This is an evidentiary assessment, not a prediction of official treatment. | Government endorsement, permission to collect competitors' staff information, or protection from investigation. |
| A lab supplies staff biographies/photos for a defined use | A written agreement can clarify permitted material, purpose and the lab's responsibilities. This addresses the collection more directly than an unrelated invoice. | That the lab holds every necessary employee permission, image licence or export authorization. Those need verification. |
| The owner takes a genuine job at a lab | An employer may support the relevant work-authorization process for actual work in China. | A job offer or employment contract does not convert the existing tourism visa into work permission or clear unrelated product activity. |

PIPL Article 13 distinguishes a contract with the individual from an employer's
necessary, lawfully governed personnel administration. A lab's business-to-business
subscription cannot simply be treated as a contract with every person profiled.
[PIPL, Article 13](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2021-08/20/c_1631050028355286.htm).
Entry and Exit Administration Law Articles 41 and 43 require applicable work
permission and residence documents and address work outside their scope.
[Work authorization rules](https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013311/content.html).

**Project assessment:** a genuine relationship can help explain what the product
does, but it should not be obtained or presented as legal cover. Its usefulness
depends on the actual scope and documents. Commercial relationships can also
produce disputes; the U.S. advisory warns of detention and exit restrictions
connected to business disputes.
[U.S. China Travel Advisory](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/china.html).

The [Dinq comparison](2026-10-02-052419-dinq-compliance-comparison.md)
examines an existing commercial service with related talent-discovery features.
Its existence does not establish approval of this project or safety of the
owner's travel.

### Travel devices are part of the practical review

The State Department warns that users should not expect network privacy in
China and notes that some travelers use devices without sensitive personal or
proprietary information. Separately, Counterespionage Law Article 25 authorizes
specified inspections of electronic equipment subject to its stated procedures.
[Travel technology guidance](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/china.html),
[Counterespionage Law, Article 25](https://www.gc.gov.cn/columns/8e15378c-0a26-4abf-b213-3a302d59a699/202311/10/d2b1c908-8e70-47d4-a5aa-d4ee83fd4806.html).

**Project assessment:** the exported HTML contains the actual images and person
records, so its travel relevance is not limited to whether an overseas database
is accessible. Counsel should review the intended devices and access alongside
the product. This is not a conclusion that possession of the export is illegal.

## Recommendations from the discussion

These are recommendations for consideration, not recorded owner decisions:

1. Obtain a PRC-qualified review covering data protection, national-security
   exposure and immigration before the next mainland trip or a material
   expansion of collection. Provide the real dossier and collection methods.
2. Keep collection focused on necessary public professional facts and editorial
   images. Retain source evidence and provide correction/removal procedures.
   Avoid expanding into private contact details, home addresses, movement
   tracking or internal company information.
3. Review the actual travel device, the dossier export and planned product work
   during the visit. Neither these precautions nor a legal review can guarantee
   a particular entry, questioning or departure outcome.

## Questions for counsel

- Where are the operator, legal entity, collectors, storage and users located?
  Will the owner run or manage collection while physically in mainland China?
- Who can access individual dossiers, and for what purposes? Distinguish
  editorial coverage, recruiting, commercial research, public APIs and other
  downstream uses rather than assuming they have the same implications.
- Which sources are lawfully public, and what permission or restrictions attach
  to photographs, logged-in platforms and third-party providers?
- Does the implemented analysis bring overseas processing within PIPL? What
  notice, rights-handling, retention, representative or cross-border obligations
  follow from the actual data flow?
- Are any institutions, projects or personnel subject to additional
  national-security or secrecy restrictions? What evidence supports that answer?
- What activities are planned during tourist visits, and what permissions are
  appropriate? How should the owner prepare devices and business records for
  lawful travel?

# Claude Japanese and Chinese: editorial assessment

Fable is the more promising voice writer in this set. Opus usually handles source
claims more carefully and supplies the stronger Chinese metro-news drafts. The
Japanese result varies by story: Opus wins the ToSpo Wang piece; Fable wins the
ToSpo Chonk piece and both complete Rocket specimens. In Chinese, Opus wins
Metro and Fable wins WeMedia for writing. Fable's WeMedia ending needs a substantive
factual repair before it could be used.

This is one editorial reading pass over the eight actual JA/CN replies, not a
prompt/process score or a native-reader certification. Rubric: hook, intended
voice, humor arising from the story, pace/coherence for unfamiliar readers, and
fidelity to sources. Both stories and all extra headlines/desk notes/shared
endings count as returned material. A combined reply is not itself a weakness.
No new generation, rewriting or page changes.

## Japanese

**ToSpo / Wang.** Opus starts with the essential reversal: “グッチ5699ドルT”
becomes “サンタクルーズの古着30ドル”. Its “値札の‘盛り’よりも、体の
‘締まり’を褒めてほしい” is native Japanese wordplay grounded in Wang's real
follow-up. The rhyme/parallel structure arises from the incident; it is not an
imported NYPost pun. The founder context is provided, and the two source images
stay separate.

Fable has more front-page theater: “1面級”, “大舞台”, “赤ペンで猛反論”. Its
thrift-store reporting-team joke is also recognizably comic: the team would like
to go, not a claim that it actually interviewed someone. But the leading total
“9569ドル疑惑” is less immediately amusing than the shirt's5699-to30 contrast.
The opening turns a light joke into a confrontation, adds a crowd's “隠れ自慢”
reaction, and states an unverified causal “火種”. These are larger narrative
additions than the playful newsroom framing alone.

Opus is the stronger complete Wang base, although it still recites more source
provenance than this small story needs. Its closing “自分で自分にツッコむ
切れ味は最先端級” is a clean final beat.

**ToSpo / Chonk.** Fable's “欧州から‘巨漢’がリングに上がった” makes the
model's size and European identity into a sports-page scene. “本紙は猫缶を
用意して待つ” completes that image. Opus's leading headline is closer to a
straight product announcement. Its best playful headline is actually among the
returned alternatives: “巨大猫AI、体重…じゃなくてパラメーター1兆”. That
alternative deserves attention; no new headline has been invented here.

Fable wins the voice comparison, with qualifications: the headline turns one
European fan's “見たか、アメリカ！” into “欧州勢が雄たけび”, and the large
performance/availability inventory delays the meme story. Both Claude models
explicitly keep the CEO naming story attributed to fans, a substantive
improvement over DeepSeek's confident naming account. Fable's desk memo connects
the two stories loosely; combining them is legitimate, but that final lesson is
less sharp than its individual cat-can joke.

**Rocket / Wang.** Fable sounds more like a companion enjoying the discovery:
“これが近所のおじさんなら『元気だな』で終わる”, “中傷（笑）”, and
“訂正への情熱の方向性が、完全に変わった”. The last line lands directly on
Wang accepting price lies if someone compliments his physique. The ordinary-man
comparison also explains why the same clothes attract attention on a celebrity,
which is useful for readers outside the AI industry.

Opus has good observations too: the glasses remain a mystery while the underwear
gets itemized, and its final return to the glasses closes the loop. But its exact
posting times, separate-image explanation and repeated statements about missing
information interrupt the narrative more often. Both models repeat “誰も聞いて
いない”; the source does not establish that nobody asked. Fable's yen conversion
is explicitly hypothetical at150yen/USD, not an observed exchange rate.

**Rocket / Chonk.** Fable begins with the mismatch between serious product naming
and a huge model called a fat cat, then ends on “名前のインパクトだけは、
いきなり最先端”. Opus's best individual joke is “巨体のわりに、動くのは一部
だけ……猫っぽい！”—a useful comic application of1Ttotal/49Bactive. But its
full technical list, chronology and long fan quotations consume the space around
that observation. Fable is the better finished specimen by a small margin;
Opus has a line worth retaining as a reference.

Both have credible surface conventions: long surprise/result headlines,
interjections, curiosity and a narrator's reaction. Long headlines alone are not
a defect. My reading of the publication's [AI experiment](https://rocketnews24.com/2026/02/02/2643910/)
and [another AI story](https://rocketnews24.com/2026/03/07/2718170/) emphasizes how
specific observations and unfolding events carry the humor. The Claude pieces
have some of that; repeated source-audit passages weaken the flow. ToSpo's own
[headline discussion](https://note.tokyo-sports.co.jp/n/ncfebac059ebb) also supports
judging the angle and useful words, not just the loudness of the headline. These
few examples inform an editorial judgment, not a claim to cover either outlet's
whole range.

## Simplified Chinese

**Metro / Wang.** Fable's “T恤5699美元？‘这是诽谤’” is a strong hook, but Opus's
headline supplies the price payoff too: “上衣30美元，二手淘的”. Opus places the
physique follow-up before detouring into the second image's different labels;
Fable makes the reader wait through both inventories. Opus also scopes240/245
to the actually priced items and does not pretend that glasses are included.

Fable is recognizable as a newspaper column, but its editor's note and invented
“南都记者” framing make the reporting feel more elaborate than the evidence
warrants. A publication-role marker is presentation; invented interviews or
facts would be a more serious problem. Here there is no invented interview,
but “记者注意到”“本报” would need truthful provenance before publication.
Its inference that contradictory labels prove all prices were made up is
stronger than the sources establish. Opus is the better full article.

**Metro / Chonk.** Opus's “参数1万亿，外号‘胖猫’” gives an outsider the whole
oddity at once. Fable's “官方昵称‘Le Chonk’” leaves the joke untranslated in the
headline. Opus also writes “计划10月底发布” in its subtitle, while Fable's
“定于” makes the plan sound settled. Both are explanatory news reports, with
less playful energy than their WeMedia counterparts. Opus is the clearer and
more economical choice.

Fable's Metro ending also says meaningful testing must wait for weights at
month-end. That does not follow: API access is already available, so third
parties can test interface behavior now. Self-hosting requires weights; all
testing does not. Its Metro naming interpretation remains conditional, unlike
the definite company-intent claim in its WeMedia conclusion.

Both Metro drafts sometimes assert more about missing independent testing than
the packet establishes. The packet says no independently reproduced result was
verified in the supplied evidence; it does not establish that the company
provided no reproducible evaluation anywhere.

**WeMedia / Wang.** Fable's “好家伙，澄清是澄清，夸还是要夸的” is direct,
colloquial and accurately identifies the source joke. The transition from a
Gucci-labelled meme to30-dollar thrift clothing and5-dollar underwear has a
natural mobile reading rhythm. Opus explains the same turn clearly, but pauses
for a large boxed caveat. Its broader premise is also followed by more biography
and explanation before the joke lands.

Fable's headline “一身Gucci” oversimplifies a mixed-brand set of false labels;
its generalizations about what big figures usually do are commentary, not facts
in the packet. Opus keeps the price subset clear but adds an unsupported claim
that joiningMeta increased Wang's attention. Fable is the livelier writing
specimen, not the cleanest final copy.

Fable also calls the translated “哈哈，不是” three characters when it contains
four Chinese characters. This is a small added error; both Claude WeMedia
drafts correctly exclude the unknown glasses price from their clothing totals.

**WeMedia / Chonk.** Fable's “胖猫不一定更能抓老鼠” is the best new Chinese
observation: it turns the parameter-count/intelligence distinction into a simple
image directly tied to this story. Opus's sequence of definitions—model,
parameters, benchmarks, API and weights—helps understanding but becomes a
technology explainer before it returns to the amusing name.

Fable's serious defect comes at the end. It first correctly attributes the
meme story to fans, then asserts “一家做万亿参数模型的公司，认认真真把网友编的
梗接了下来，写进了官方发布帖”, followed by “这不是什么公关技巧，就是一种
松弛感”. The first asserts unconfirmed naming intent; the second supplies an
unverified motive and rules out a PR explanation. The earlier caveat does not
repair the ending. Opus keeps the motive unresolved and explicitly enjoys the
nickname anyway. Fable wins voice; Opus wins evidential discipline.

## Relation to the earlier results

Claude adds useful voice choices and specific new jokes, especially Fable's
Chonk metaphors and Opus's Japanese“盛り／締まり”. The strongest earlierGPT6.1
samples still deliver several of these stories more economically: nativeRocket
Wang's “眼鏡より下着の情報が詳しい”, earlierToSpo's precise shirt-price
headline and ending, and earlierChineseWeMedia's “价格可以谈，夸人请继续” /
“名字负责卖萌，参数负责撑场面”. I would keep those as reference specimens,
not declare an across-the-board Claude victory.

Relative to DeepSeek, the two Claude models supply assessable Rocket articles
and more careful Japanese fan attribution. That is better coverage/fidelity in
this trial; DeepSeek's refusal does not measure its unobserved writing ability.
For ChineseWeMedia, Fable has a stronger story-specific cat joke, but shares the
problem of turning a fan account into a confident concluding explanation.

For these small Chatter stories, the recurring editing problem is low payoff
from long supplier-specification blocks, inventories of a second photo's brands,
exact posting times and repeated caveats. The necessary founder/company/context
explanation should remain. More words are useful when they add another scene,
observation or joke; several passages only repeat what the reader already knows.
No automatic edits are made by this assessment.
